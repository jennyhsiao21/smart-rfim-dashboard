The Problem

Most merchants ran customer segmentation in a spreadsheet — or not at all.

Across 15,000 enterprise merchants on the SHOPLINE engagement platform, only the most sophisticated had data teams running RFM analyses. The other 90% had untapped revenue sitting in dormant cohorts and no scalable way to reach them.

⚙

Manual workflows didn't scale

Top-decile merchants segmented customers in Excel. The bottom 90% relied on broad blasts to all customers, watching unsubscribe rates climb and conversion fall.

⏱

Industry RFM ignored social

Classic Recency–Frequency–Monetary models treat every customer who hasn't purchased as silent. But social-commerce customers leave signals: livestream comments, post engagements, DMs. RFM left those unused.

📉

Activation was disconnected from segmentation

Even merchants who built segments couldn't act on them — the marketing tool, the rewards tool, and the export tool were three separate workflows. Segments became reports, not levers.

Mar 2026 update

The model evolved. The original 9-segment model included a "Loyal Customers" segment, but post-launch data showed it overlapped messily with "Top Shoppers" — merchants found segments hard to act on cleanly. With the 2026 release, Loyal moved to a custom-filter workflow (Order count > N AND Spend = High) and Top Shoppers split into Core Top Shopper (still actively buying) and Inactive Top Shopper (high lifetime value but starting to slow). The split surfaces the highest-ROI reactivation cohort the previous model was hiding.

The 9-Segment Heatmap

Two axes: repurchase cycle × lifetime value.

Every customer is plotted on a heatmap. The X-axis is where they sit in the merchant's repurchase cycle (Recently purchased / Ready-to-repurchase / Lapsed). The Y-axis is their lifetime purchase tier (Top Shopper / Repeated / One-time / Browser). The intersection produces 9 distinct, non-overlapping segments — each with its own activation playbook.

Cycle thresholds: Recently purchased = ≤ 0.8× store cycle · Ready-to-repurchase = 0.8–1.2× cycle · Lapsed = > 1.2× cycle · Continued Engagement = interaction within 3× cycle.

Y-axis thresholds: Top Shopper = (purchases > 3 OR top 10% purchase count) OR spend > top 10% · Repeated = 1 < purchases ≤ top tier · One-time = 1 purchase · Browser = 0 purchases with interaction.

How It Works

Per-merchant cycle, not global thresholds.

Every merchant gets their own repurchase cycle calculated from their repeat-shopper history. A "Lapsed" customer for a fast-fashion brand (cycle = 14 days) is a different concept from a "Lapsed" customer for furniture (cycle = 180 days). The model adapts.

01

Compute the merchant's repurchase cycle

Median number of days between purchases across all repeat shoppers in the past 2 years. New merchants (<6mo) inherit the category median to avoid cold-start.

02

Bucket each customer on the X-axis

Recently purchased ≤ 0.8× cycle · Ready-to-repurchase 0.8–1.2× cycle · Lapsed > 1.2× cycle. Cycle-relative, not absolute.

03

Bucket each customer on the Y-axis

Top Shopper / Repeated / One-time / Browser — driven by purchase count thresholds AND lifetime spend top-10%. The OR-condition logic prevents segment overlap.

04

Apply interaction rule for ambiguous cases

Lapsed customers with interaction within 3× cycle stay reachable (Ready-to-Repurchase / Potential). Lapsed without interaction become Sleeping / Lost.

05

Refresh daily

Daily incremental refresh. Stability over raw accuracy — merchants need segments they can plan a quarter-long campaign around without whiplash.

-- Smart RFIM segmentation, simplified
-- Updated Mar 2026: Loyal removed, Top split into Core/Inactive
WITH store_cycle AS (
  SELECT merchant_id,
    PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (
      ORDER BY days_between_orders
    ) AS cycle_days
  FROM repeat_purchase_intervals
  GROUP BY merchant_id
),
customer_metrics AS (
  SELECT o.merchant_id, o.customer_id,
    COUNT(*) AS purchases,
    SUM(o.gmv_usd) AS total_spend,
    CURRENT_DATE - MAX(o.order_at)::date AS recency_days
  FROM orders o
  WHERE o.auth_status = 'success'
  GROUP BY o.merchant_id, o.customer_id
)
SELECT cm.*, sc.cycle_days,
  CASE
    -- Top tier × cycle position
    WHEN top_shopper AND recency_days <= 0.8*cycle_days
      THEN 'Core Top Shopper'
    WHEN top_shopper AND recency_days > 0.8*cycle_days
      THEN 'Inactive Top Shopper'
    -- Repeated tier × cycle position
    WHEN repeated AND recency_days <= 0.8*cycle_days
      THEN 'Active Shopper'
    WHEN repeated AND recency_days <= 1.2*cycle_days
      THEN 'Ready-to-Repurchase'
    WHEN repeated AND has_interaction
      THEN 'Ready-to-Repurchase'
    WHEN repeated
      THEN 'Sleeping Shopper'
    -- One-time tier × cycle position
    WHEN one_time AND recency_days <= 0.8*cycle_days
      THEN 'New Shopper'
    WHEN one_time AND (recency_days <= 1.2*cycle_days
      OR has_interaction) THEN 'Potential Shopper'
    WHEN one_time THEN 'Lost Shopper'
    -- Browsers
    WHEN has_interaction THEN 'Engaged Customer'
  END AS rfim_segment
FROM customer_metrics cm
JOIN store_cycle sc USING (merchant_id);

Customer Story

One merchant. One week. Half a million in incremental revenue.

Bonny & Read Taiwan-based fashion accessories brand

The Sleeping Shopper reactivation play

Bonny & Read had a long tail of customers who'd bought once and gone quiet. Before Smart RFIM, reactivating them meant a marketing manager pulling lists by hand, segmenting in Excel, and crafting bespoke offers — work the team didn't have headcount for.

With Smart RFIM, they segmented the Sleeping Shopper cohort by depth of dormancy and lifetime value, then issued differentiated store-credit offers — heavier credit to the highest-value, deepest-sleeping customers. Single workflow, no spreadsheets.

"Don't have to pull customer reports or send store credit by hand any more. Saved a lot of headcount cost. Results are excellent."
